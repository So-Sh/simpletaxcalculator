Say you inherit $100,000. If you are a friend of the person who died, you could owe $15,000 in Pennsylvania or New Jersey, around $10,000 to $12,700 in Maryland, Kentucky, or Nebraska, and nothing in the 45 states with no inheritance tax. Your relationship to the person who died decides most of it.

This guide shows what each of the five inheritance-tax states would charge, how each system works, and which state's rules apply to you. Rates and exemptions reflect state rules in effect as of August 2026, with Kentucky checked against the law as amended in April 2026. We skip the basic definitions here. For how inheritance tax differs from estate tax, read our [inheritance tax vs estate tax guide](/blog/inheritance-tax-vs-estate-tax).

These figures assume one $100,000 bequest to one person, with no other transfers. They also assume the adult child is over 21 (Pennsylvania exempts a child 21 or younger inheriting from a parent) and that the heir is 22 or older (Nebraska exempts younger heirs entirely). Your own result will differ if your situation does not fit.

Here is how each number works:

- **Pennsylvania:** a flat rate on the full amount. 4.5% for an adult child, 12% for a sibling, 15% for everyone else.
- **New Jersey:** a sibling is Class C, so the first $25,000 is exempt and the rest is taxed at 11%: ($100,000 − $25,000) × 11% = $8,250. Nieces, nephews, and friends are Class D, taxed at 15% from the first dollar.
- **Nebraska:** a niece or nephew gets a $40,000 exemption, then 11%: $60,000 × 11% = $6,600. A friend gets a $25,000 exemption, then 15%: $75,000 × 15% = $11,250. Children and siblings have a $100,000 exemption, so nothing is owed at this amount.
- **Kentucky:** a friend is Class C. The bracket tax is $12,700, less $30 for the $500 exemption, which comes to $12,670. Details are in the Kentucky section below.
- **Maryland:** a flat 10% on collateral heirs: $100,000 × 10% = $10,000.

A graduated rate applies only to the part of the inheritance inside each bracket. It is not the rate you pay on the whole amount.

<a id="pennsylvania"></a>
## Pennsylvania: the simplest system

Pennsylvania has no exemption threshold. The tax applies from the first dollar to any beneficiary who is not exempt. The rate depends only on who you are:

- **Spouse:** 0%.
- **Child 21 or younger, inheriting from a parent:** 0%.
- **Parent, inheriting from a child 21 or younger:** 0%.
- **Adult children, grandchildren, stepchildren, parents, and grandparents:** 4.5%, unless one of the exemptions above applies.
- **Siblings, including half-siblings:** 12%.
- **Everyone else, including nieces, nephews, friends, and unmarried partners:** 15%.
- **Qualified charities and government entities:** 0%.

If the tax is paid within three months of the date of death, Pennsylvania gives a 5% discount on the tax due. The return is due within nine months. See our [Pennsylvania inheritance tax page](/pennsylvania/inheritance-tax) for the full calculator.

<a id="new-jersey"></a>
## New Jersey inheritance tax rates

New Jersey sorts heirs into classes and taxes transfers of $500 or more:

- **Class A:** spouse, civil union or domestic partner, children, stepchildren, grandchildren, parents, and grandparents. Fully exempt.
- **Class C:** siblings and sons- and daughters-in-law. The first $25,000 is exempt. Then 11% up to $1,100,000, 13% up to $1,400,000, 14% up to $1,700,000, and 16% above that.
- **Class D:** everyone else, including nieces, nephews, cousins, friends, and unmarried partners. No exemption. 15% on the first $700,000 and 16% above that.
- **Class E:** charities, government entities, and religious or educational institutions. Fully exempt.

New Jersey's highest inheritance-tax rate is 16%, but that does not mean a sibling pays 16%. For Class C, 16% applies only to the part above $1,700,000.

Friends and unmarried partners are the most exposed. A long-term partner who is not a spouse or registered partner is Class D, so a $100,000 inheritance costs $15,000. A spouse would owe nothing. The return is due within eight months, and there is no early-payment discount. Try the [New Jersey inheritance tax calculator](/new-jersey/inheritance-tax) to run your own numbers.

## Inheritance tax in Nebraska: big exemptions, collected by counties

Nebraska has the largest exemptions of the five states, and it is the only one where the county collects the tax. You pay it to the county treasurer where the person who died lived, or where the real estate is located. The rates and exemptions are still set by state law.

<a id="kentucky"></a>
## Kentucky: the one that just changed

Kentucky has three classes, and the exemptions are small:

- **Class A:** fully exempt. This includes the spouse, parents, children, stepchildren, grandchildren, and siblings (including half-siblings). Since the 2026 change, it also includes nieces and nephews.
- **Class B:** sons- and daughters-in-law, aunts and uncles, and great-grandchildren. $1,000 exemption, then graduated rates from 4% to 16%.
- **Class C:** everyone else, including cousins, friends, and most organizations. $500 exemption, then graduated rates from 6% to 16%.

<Callout type="info" title="Kentucky changed its law in 2026">
  <p>Under amendments effective April 27, 2026, nieces and nephews (including half-nieces and half-nephews) are Class A and fully exempt. The change applies to estates of people who died on or after January 1, 2026. For earlier deaths, the previous version of the law generally applies, and nieces and nephews were Class B. Older guides may still show the old classes.</p>
</Callout>

The exemption comes off the lowest bracket. In practice, that cuts the tax by $40 for Class B (4% of $1,000) or $30 for Class C (6% of $500).

<ExampleBlock label="Example">
A friend inherits $100,000 from a Kentucky resident. That is Class C. The tax is $600 on the first $10,000, $800 on the next $10,000, $1,000 on the next $10,000, $1,800 on the next $15,000, $2,100 on the next $15,000, and $6,400 on the remaining $40,000. That adds up to $12,700. The $500 exemption removes $30, so the tax is $12,670.

An aunt or uncle (Class B) inheriting the same $100,000 would owe $9,000 in bracket tax, less $40, for $8,960.
</ExampleBlock>

Kentucky gives a 5% discount if the tax is paid within nine months of death. The return is due within 18 months. Kentucky has no estate tax, so the inheritance tax is the only state-level death tax. See our [Kentucky inheritance tax page](/kentucky/inheritance-tax) for more.

<a id="maryland"></a>
## Maryland: inheritance tax plus estate tax

Maryland is the only state that has both an inheritance tax and a separate estate tax. They work differently.

**The inheritance tax** is simple. Spouses, registered domestic partners, parents, grandparents, children, stepchildren, grandchildren, siblings, and sons- and daughters-in-law are exempt. Everyone else, called collateral heirs, pays a flat 10% on the value of what they receive. That covers nieces, nephews, aunts, uncles, cousins, friends, and unmarried partners. Inheritances of $1,000 or less per person are exempt.

**The estate tax** is paid by the estate before anything is distributed. It has a $5 million exemption per individual, which is not adjusted for inflation, and a top rate of 16%. The $5 million threshold applies only to the estate tax. It is not an inheritance tax exemption.

The two taxes overlap, but Maryland credits inheritance tax paid against estate tax owed, so the same assets are not fully taxed twice. The return is due within nine months. See our [Maryland inheritance tax page](/maryland/inheritance-tax) for details.

<a id="which-state"></a>
## Which state's rules apply to you?

Which state's inheritance tax applies generally depends on where the person who died lived and where certain property is located, not where the heir lives.

For example, if your aunt lived in Pennsylvania, her estate may owe Pennsylvania inheritance tax even if you live in Texas. If she lived in a state with no inheritance tax, you generally would not owe one just because you live in a state that has it. Property can change this. Real estate in a taxing state may be taxable there even if the owner lived elsewhere. Kentucky's statute, for example, has rules for property of people who died as nonresidents and prorates the exemption for them, and Nebraska allows payment to the county where real estate is located.

Federal rules are separate. There is no federal inheritance tax. The federal estate tax is paid by the estate, and it only applies above the exemption, which is $15 million per person for 2026 ($30 million for a married couple using portability). Fewer than 1% of estates owe it.

If you inherit assets like stocks or a house and later sell them, a different tax can apply. Inherited assets generally get a stepped-up cost basis, which can shrink the capital gain. Our [capital gains calculation guide](/blog/how-capital-gains-tax-is-calculated) explains how that works.

## The bottom line

Only five states charge an inheritance tax, and in every one of them a surviving spouse pays nothing. Beyond that, the bill depends on who you are to the person who died. Children and siblings are often exempt or taxed lightly, while friends and unmarried partners can face the highest rates, from 10% to 16%. Kentucky changed its rules in 2026, and Maryland adds a separate estate tax on top.
